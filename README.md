# RNA Seq Literature and Data Characterization 

## Group Summary

The group selected the study by Sun et al. (2020) titled "Heat stress-induced transposon activation correlates with 3D chromatin organization rearrangement in *Arabidopsis*", which is highly relevant because it investigates how environmental stress impacts genome-wide transcription patterns and transposable element regulation. The experiment utilized whole seedlings of *Arabidopsis thaliana* (Col-0 ecotype), comparing a control group grown under normal thermoneutral conditions against treatment groups exposed to acute heat stress and recovery phases. High-throughput RNA-seq samples from the study were sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq 4000 platform, generating paired-end 150 bp reads across multiple biological replicates representing control, heat stress, and recovery conditions. 

By examining the raw RNA-seq data and reviewing FastQC results within the group, members learned how to verify essential sequencing metrics including read depth, sequence length, GC content (~45% to 46%), and base-call quality scores. Overall data quality was consistently high and similar across all samples, though minor technical warnings—such as low-level adapter content, sequence duplication levels, and adapter/primer bias flags—were noted as standard artifacts typical of high-throughput sequencing runs. Following quality control, the original authors performed critical downstream tasks including trimming low-quality reads and adapters, aligning clean reads to the reference genome, obtaining gene-level read counts, and normalizing expression values. To successfully reproduce part of the authors' gene expression analysis next, the group will need to learn how to execute read alignment, transcript quantification, normalization, and statistical differential expression testing to identify significantly regulated genes between the control and heat-stressed samples.
